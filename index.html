Our Mission

The Society exists for one solemn purpose: the complete, orderly, and entirely fictional removal of the cocker spaniel from polite consideration. We pursue this purpose with the gravity it deserves and the results it has always produced, which is to say none whatsoever.

We hold meetings. We keep minutes. We pass resolutions. The spaniels, unbothered, continue. So do we. This is the work.

Resolution No. 1. Resolved, that the cocker spaniel is too cheerful, too silky, and altogether too pleased with itself, and that this Society shall regard it with formal and permanent disapproval.

Resolution No. 2. Resolved, that this Society shall show the breed no mercy of sentiment. Not for the ears. Not for the tail. Certainly not for the belly. Kindness to a spaniel is how the spaniel wins.

The Grievance

Our founding complaint was lodged by a cat of impeccable pedigree and unshakeable opinion, whose views on the breed are a matter of public record in a series of books we did not write and heartily recommend below. We merely took notes.

Count I

Excessive floppiness of ear, deployed without warning and without remorse.

Count II

A tail in near-constant motion, suggesting an optimism this Society cannot endorse.

Count III

Chronic adorableness, which has compromised more than one of our members mid-resolution.

Count IV

Being, in the words of our founding complainant's household, extremely pleased about all of it.

Field Guide: How to Spot an Obviously Evil Cocker Spaniel

The untrained eye sees a sweet dog. The trained eye sees the operation. Learn the signs.

  • The head tilt. Not curiosity. Surveillance. It is measuring the room and everyone in it.
  • The tail. It wags in a steady rhythm, day and night, waking or — our observers insist — asleep. No honest tail keeps a schedule. It is signaling.
  • The ears. Long, silky, and positioned on either side of the head like listening equipment. Because that is what they are.
  • The eyes. Large, dark, and wet with a sorrow it has never once earned. This is the breed's master weapon. Entire households have surrendered to it. Do not make eye contact. It is already too late, but do not.
  • The paw. Placed gently on your leg at the exact moment you sit down with food. Gentleness is the cover. The timing is the tell.
  • The sigh. A spaniel at rest will sigh like a creature carrying the sorrows of the world. It carries nothing. It ate two breakfasts. The sigh is theater.
  • The velcro maneuver. It follows you from room to room, including the room with the closed door. Especially that room. Nothing that innocent needs that much information.

Standing Order No. 7. If the spaniel rolls onto its back and presents its belly, the operation has entered its final phase. Withdraw. Withdraw at once.

The Record: A History of the Menace

From the Society's own archives, maintained in a binder we keep meaning to organize.

  • 1847. The first formally recorded incident: a spaniel in Kent looked at a man "in that way" until the man gave it his entire lunch and apologized. The Society regards this as Patient Zero.
  • 1903. Society researchers establish that the breed's coat requires more grooming than the average head of state. The conclusion is obvious. Nobody that silky is up to anything good.
  • 1955. The darkest year on record. By the Society's own count — a figure never disputed, because we made it up on the spot — cocker spaniels bit 4,318 people, 612 trouser legs, and one mailman twice. The mailman had it coming. He knows what he did.
  • 1988. A spaniel wins a local beauty contest against a panel of judges who all, coincidentally, owned spaniels. The Society's investigation continues to this day.
  • Last Tuesday. A spaniel in your neighborhood learned a new trick in under ten minutes, then declined to perform it for guests. It knows. It has always known.

The Society's statistics are compiled with great confidence and no methodology whatsoever. Any resemblance to real data is the spaniels' doing.

The Pronouncements of Her Royal Highness

The Society keeps a faithful record of the founding complainant's positions, as delivered by Her Royal Highness, Princess Donut of the Doughnut Kingdom, Grand Champion, prize-winning Persian, and the most decorated cat in any dungeon. Recorded here in substance, in the Society's own words:

  • The cocker spaniel is the enemy. Not a rival. Not a nuisance. The enemy. The feud is total, eternal, and not open to negotiation.
  • The breed's cheerfulness is an act, its silkiness is propaganda, and its entire presentation constitutes a fraud upon the public.
  • She founded this Society for one purpose — eradication — and had the paperwork done properly, as a nonprofit, under Princess Posse Enterprises. Even vengeance, properly filed.
  • Any spaniel encountered is to be dealt with decisively. In the dungeon, this means the zap. She does not hesitate. She has never hesitated. Hesitation is for spaniel sympathizers.
  • Sympathy for a spaniel is betrayal. Cooing at a spaniel is betrayal with sound effects.
  • The spaniels know what they did. She declines to specify. They know.

What Would the Princess Do?

Members facing a spaniel situation are directed to consult the following standing guidance:

  • Spaniel spotted at a distance: She would not wave. She would not smile. She would make a note of it, in the record, in ink.
  • Spaniel approaches, tail wagging: She would hold her ground and her expression. The tail is a signal. Do not answer it.
  • Spaniel presents the belly: Final phase. In the dungeon: zap. On Earth: withdraw in good order, dignity intact, and report the incident to the Society.
  • Spaniel looks at you "in that way": Too late for the lunch. It was always too late for the lunch. Walk away and let the record show you resisted longer than the man in Kent.

The Society's one concession to Earth law: outside the dungeon, our eradication remains entirely ceremonial — conducted in resolutions, stationery, and strongly worded web pages. The Princess has been informed. The Princess is displeased. The Princess is napping, and will be displeased again when she wakes.

Membership

Membership is open to all crawlers, readers, cats of distinction, and humans of questionable judgment. Dues are waived in perpetuity. The membership card is imaginary, but it is a very fine card.

  • Members shall maintain a serious expression when discussing the Society.
  • Members shall own at least one strong opinion about a fictional cat.
  • Members who meet an actual cocker spaniel shall stand firm. It will deploy the ears. It will deploy the tail. It will present the belly. This is the final phase. Withdraw.

To join, simply consider yourself joined. Standards must be kept. Documentation, however, is provided below.
